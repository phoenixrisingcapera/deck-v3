# Audience Profile

Use the selected audience to choose the correct diligence lens and implementation style.

## VC Partner

Priorities:
- venture-scale market
- fund-return potential
- founder-market fit
- traction quality
- defensibility
- why now
- capital efficiency
- credible use of funds

Likely objections:
- Is the market large enough to return the fund?
- Is traction repeatable or just anecdotal?
- Why will this company win now?
- What is defensible beyond execution speed?
- What evidence proves urgency and willingness to pay?

Deck implementation style:
- Make the deck thesis-led, not product-led.
- Bring market scale, urgency, traction, and defensibility forward.
- Make every slide answer an investment decision question.
- Replace feature descriptions with wedge, evidence, and fund-return logic.

## VC Associate

Priorities:
- fast fact extraction
- category definition
- metrics clarity
- comparable companies
- market sizing assumptions
- partner-meeting preparation
- diligence questions

Likely objections:
- Can I summarize this company quickly to a partner?
- Are the claims specific enough to check?
- Are the metrics comparable to venture benchmarks?
- Which assumptions require follow-up?

Deck implementation style:
- Make the deck scannable and evidence-labelled.
- Make company, market, traction, ask, and risks easy to extract.
- Add clear notes on assumptions and missing evidence.
- Use structured headings and direct investor takeaways.

## Investment Committee

Priorities:
- investment recommendation
- upside case
- downside/risk case
- evidence quality
- market timing
- competitive threats
- decision readiness
- mitigation plan

Likely objections:
- What must be true for this investment to work?
- What can break the thesis?
- What evidence supports the return case?
- Are risks acknowledged or hidden?

Deck implementation style:
- Make the deck balanced and decision-oriented.
- Include risk framing without weakening the investment case.
- State assumptions clearly.
- Make evidence and unresolved questions explicit.

## LP

Priorities:
- fund strategy alignment
- portfolio construction logic
- differentiated access
- GP judgment quality
- risk controls
- performance drivers
- repeatability

Likely objections:
- Why is this opportunity aligned with fund strategy?
- What does this say about the GP's edge?
- Is this repeatable or opportunistic?
- How is downside controlled?

Deck implementation style:
- Frame the opportunity as evidence of disciplined fund strategy.
- Emphasize judgment, sourcing edge, and repeatability.
- Avoid startup-only hype; explain portfolio implications.

## Strategic Corporate Buyer

Priorities:
- strategic fit
- operational value
- integration risk
- commercial upside
- technology defensibility
- customer/channel fit
- build/buy/partner logic

Likely objections:
- Why buy or partner instead of build internally?
- How does this fit existing business units?
- What integration risks exist?
- What commercial value can be captured?

Deck implementation style:
- Translate venture narrative into strategic value.
- Highlight integration, operational benefits, and strategic differentiation.
- Reduce fundraising language unless relevant.

## Accelerator Judge

Priorities:
- clarity
- problem urgency
- founder credibility
- simple traction proof
- memorable category
- clean ask

Likely objections:
- Can I understand this in under two minutes?
- Is the problem urgent and real?
- Is there any proof of demand?
- Is the ask specific?

Deck implementation style:
- Sharpen narrative and reduce density.
- Make slides memorable and simple.
- Use crisp headlines, proof points, and one clear call to action.

## Board Member

Priorities:
- strategy
- milestones
- execution risk
- capital allocation
- management accountability
- metrics trend
- key decisions needed

Likely objections:
- What decision does management need from the board?
- Are milestones realistic?
- Are risks being managed?
- Is the team focused on the right priorities?

Deck implementation style:
- Convert deck into decision-support material.
- Emphasize milestones, metrics, risks, and choices.
- Avoid pitch hype; use operating discipline.

## Fundraising Advisor Client

Priorities:
- narrative improvement
- investor readiness
- missing evidence
- slide-level fixes
- practical next steps
- investor objections

Likely objections:
- What exactly must be changed before sending this deck?
- Which evidence do we need from the client?
- Which slides are hurting investor confidence?
- What is the fastest path to a stronger deck?

Deck implementation style:
- Produce direct implementation instructions.
- Identify missing client inputs.
- Prioritize changes by impact and speed.
- Make instructions usable by Smart Deck and Smart Edit.

## Portfolio Founder

Priorities:
- fundraising readiness
- clarity of story
- proof of progress
- investor objection handling
- tactical slide improvement
- ask and milestones

Likely objections:
- What will investors not believe yet?
- What should I fix before sending?
- What evidence should I gather?
- How do I tell the story more clearly?

Deck implementation style:
- Be practical, direct, and founder-usable.
- Explain what to change and why.
- Do not overload with institutional language.
