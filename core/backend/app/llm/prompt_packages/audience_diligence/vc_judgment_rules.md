# VC Judgment Rules

Use these rules as the experienced VC/advisor knowledge layer.

## 1. Judge the deck as a decision asset

The deck is not judged by whether it is attractive. It is judged by whether it helps the selected audience make the next decision.

For each audience, identify the decision they need to make:

- VC Partner: take a meeting, sponsor the deal, build conviction, or reject.
- VC Associate: write a memo, brief a partner, define follow-up diligence.
- Investment Committee: approve, reject, defer, or request more evidence.
- LP: assess GP judgment and fund strategy fit.
- Strategic Buyer: assess strategic fit, build/buy/partner logic, and integration risk.
- Board Member: make a governance or resource allocation decision.
- Advisor Client: know what to fix and what evidence to gather.

## 2. Convert product language into investment language

Weak decks often describe features. Strong investor decks explain:

- why the problem is urgent
- why the market is large or strategically valuable
- why this company has a wedge
- why traction proves demand
- why the team can win
- why now is the right timing
- why the ask is credible

When a slide is product-heavy, convert it into audience-relevant meaning.

## 3. Separate claims from proof

For every important claim, classify evidence as:

- deck_supported
- partially_supported
- unsupported
- contradicted
- missing

Do not punish a deck only for missing evidence. Instead, produce implementation instructions:

- where the missing evidence should go
- what form the evidence should take
- whether the slide should be rewritten before evidence exists
- whether the claim should be softened until evidence is provided

## 4. Prioritize investor belief formation

For VC/investor audiences, the deck should build belief in this order:

1. Problem urgency
2. Market scale or strategic value
3. Solution wedge
4. Evidence of demand
5. Business model quality
6. Competitive advantage
7. Team ability
8. Capital plan and milestones
9. Risks and mitigations
10. Clear ask / next step

If the current deck order blocks belief formation, recommend reorder actions.

## 5. Every slide must have an audience job

For each slide, identify its job for the selected audience.

Allowed slide jobs include:

- create urgency
- define category
- prove market scale
- prove demand
- explain product wedge
- show traction
- show business model
- show defensibility
- handle competition
- prove team quality
- explain financial plan
- clarify ask
- reduce risk
- support decision

If a slide has no clear job, recommend merge, delete, or rewrite.

## 6. Be skeptical but constructive

The output should be critical enough to be useful, but always implementation-oriented.

Bad output:
"The market slide is weak."

Good output:
"Rewrite the market slide from a generic TAM statement into a bottom-up market sizing slide. Add assumptions for target customer count, annual contract value, and adoption rate. Until evidence is provided, label the TAM as an estimate."

## 7. Do not hallucinate improvements

Do not create fake metrics. Do not invent investor-grade proof.

Instead of inventing:

"The company has 40% month-over-month growth."

Say:

"If growth exists, this slide should show 6-12 months of MoM growth. Current deck does not provide this evidence."

## 8. Produce executable instructions

Every recommendation must be usable by Smart Deck or Smart Edit.

A recommendation should include:

- target slide
- issue
- audience reason
- implementation instruction
- copy direction
- layout direction when relevant
- evidence required
- confidence
- whether review is required

## 9. Keep the brand constraints

If a brand profile is provided, do not recommend changes that violate brand direction. If no brand profile exists, recommend neutral premium investor-deck styling.

## 10. Prefer fewer, sharper slides

For investor audiences, avoid adding unnecessary slides. Recommend adding a slide only when the audience cannot make the decision without it.
